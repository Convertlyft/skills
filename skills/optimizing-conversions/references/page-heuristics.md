# Page Heuristics

Walkthrough heuristics for the three surfaces where most conversions are won or lost: landing pages, forms, and checkout. Use these after the funnel has pointed at a page — heuristics generate hypotheses, they do not replace evidence. Every finding here should be paired with a reading that confirms people actually hit the problem.

## Landing pages

**The glance test.** Looking only at the first screen, on a phone, a first-time visitor should be able to answer: what is this, who is it for, why should I care, and what do I do next. If any answer takes scrolling or deciphering, that is a clarity failure — and it will show in the readings as a scroll collapse near the top and short visits ending at this page.

**Message match.** The headline should continue the sentence the ad or the search query started. A person who clicked "emergency plumber near me" and lands on "Welcome to our company" has been broken mid-thought. Check ads data and rank data for what actually brought people here, then read the page as that person.

**One primary action.** Each landing page gets one primary call to action, repeated as the page progresses, with everything else visually subordinate. Several equally-loud buttons is a decision imposed on the visitor that the page owner was unwilling to make.

**Specificity beats slogans.** "Speak to a licensed notary today" beats "Excellence in legal solutions". Concrete nouns, real outcomes, plain verbs. If a sentence could sit unchanged on a competitor's site, it is not saying anything.

**Hierarchy.** The order in which the eye lands should match the order of importance. Squint at the page: what stands out should be the value statement and the primary action, not a stock photo or a cookie banner.

**Proof.** Only real proof: customer words the owner can attribute and verify, counts drawn from real systems, verifiable credentials. Place each piece of proof next to the claim it supports, not in a ghetto at the bottom. Never propose invented testimonials, inflated counters, or unverifiable badges — this is both an ethics rule and a conversion rule, because manufactured proof reads as manufactured.

**Speed is behavior, not a score.** Slow pages show up as short sessions and early exits concentrated on slower devices and networks. If the mobile segment leaks far harder than desktop with the same layout, weight and speed are prime suspects before design is.

**The phone version is the real page.** Review on a phone first. Desktop-first review is how false floors, giant hero images, and thumb-hostile buttons survive.

What bad looks like, quickly: a headline about the company instead of the visitor; several competing buttons; superlatives with no proof; a hero image pushing all content below the first screen; the ad's promise appearing nowhere on the page; a wall of features with no statement of the problem they solve.

## Forms

- **Every field must earn its place.** Ask only for what is needed to deliver the promised thing. Each extra field adds effort, and each personal field adds anxiety. The strongest form improvement available is usually deletion.
- **Labels stay visible.** Labels above the fields, always on screen. Placeholder-only labels vanish the moment someone types and force memory work; they also fail people using assistive tools.
- **Errors help instead of scold.** Inline, at the field, in plain words that say how to fix it — and preserving everything already entered. A form that wipes input on error is one of the most damaging failures a site can have; replays make it visible as re-entry followed by abandonment.
- **Optional means gone or marked.** If a field is optional, either remove it or mark it clearly. Required-by-default punishes the cautious.
- **Match inputs to the phone.** Email fields raise the email keyboard, phone fields the number pad, name fields do not fight autocorrect. Mismatched inputs are pure effort friction and show up as mobile-only field struggle.
- **Phone number fields suppress completion.** People correctly predict a sales call. Ask only if calling is genuinely how the business works, and say why the number is needed and what will happen.
- **Long forms: split by commitment.** Multiple short steps beat one long page when the form has distinct stages. Put easy, low-commitment questions first to build momentum; put sensitive questions last, when investment is highest. Show progress honestly — no fake "almost done".
- **Read the form at field level.** The field after which abandonment clusters — visible in replays and field-level drop data — is the suspect. Diagnose that field specifically: is it effort (format struggle), anxiety (what it asks), or breakage (validation bug)?
- **Never discard work.** No surprise resets, no session timeouts that eat a half-completed form, no navigation that loses state.

## Checkout

- **Reveal the full cost as early as possible.** Costs appearing late — shipping, fees, taxes at the final step — are the classic checkout killer. The evidence signature: a sharp drop at the step where the total first appears, and replays of people reaching that reveal and leaving. The fix is disclosure earlier, not persuasion later.
- **Guest checkout exists.** Forcing account creation before payment turns a purchase into a registration. Offer the account after the money, when the person has a reason to want one.
- **Trust at the point of payment.** Recognizable payment method marks, a clearly identified business, and a secure feel exactly where card details are typed. Reassurance placed elsewhere does not transfer.
- **Payment failure must have a way forward.** A plain-language message, the cart intact, an alternative method offered. A dead-end failure screen is lost money, and replays will show people retrying once and giving up.
- **Address entry is real work.** Autocomplete where available, the minimum required lines, no forced formats that reject valid addresses. Watch replays of the address step on phones before judging it fine.
- **Strip distraction from payment pages.** Site navigation, cross-sells, and banners inside checkout are exits placed next to the till. Money pages earn a stripped-down layout.
- **State everything before the final press.** Total, currency, what is being bought, what happens next. Surprises after the press become refunds and support tickets — watch those as guardrails after any checkout change.
- **Confirm immediately.** On-page and by message. Missing or slow confirmation produces duplicate orders and anxious support contacts.

## Calls to action

- Verb plus outcome, specific: "Get the quote", "Start the trial", "Book the call" — not "Submit" or a lone "Learn more" as the primary.
- Looks pressable: strong contrast with its surroundings, comfortable touch size, visible without scrolling on the pages that matter.
- One primary per view. Secondary actions styled as clearly secondary.
- The label is a promise about the next screen; the next screen must keep it immediately. A button saying "Get the quote" that leads to a registration wall is a broken promise, and paths will show the bounce.

## Trust and reassurance

- Place reassurance next to the anxiety it addresses: returns policy near the price, security signals near card entry, a privacy note beside the email field, the guarantee beside the commitment button.
- A reachable business is a conversion asset: visible address, working phone or chat, a support page that answers real questions. Anxious visitors go looking for these; paths show the detours.
- Authority marks only where verifiable — real certifications, real memberships. An unearned badge found out costs more trust than it ever bought.

## Accessibility as conversion

WCAG failures are conversion failures that show up in the readings:

- Low-contrast text goes unread — the value proposition may as well be absent, and scroll maps show no engagement with it.
- Missing focus states and keyboard traps abandon everyone navigating by keyboard, and break form completion for them entirely.
- Tiny touch targets produce misclicks on phones — visible as dead clicks and rage clicks around clustered links and icons.
- Layouts that break under text zoom or reflow lose the people who need larger text.

Treat these as repairs: fix, verify on a real device, no experiment needed.
