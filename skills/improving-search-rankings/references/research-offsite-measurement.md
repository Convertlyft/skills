# Research, off-site, and measurement — keywords, SERPs, links, tracking, local, AI search, Ads overlap

Scope: choosing what to rank for, judging links, reading rank data honestly,
local search, AI-search visibility, and where Google Ads quality overlaps
with SEO.

## Keyword research — a method, not a tool report

The goal is a short list of queries the site can realistically win that
people who would pay actually search. Method:

1. **Seed from reality, not imagination.** Sources in order of trust: the
   Ads search-terms report (what real people typed before converting — the
   single best source when Ads data exists), Search Console queries the site
   already almost ranks for, what customers say verbatim in emails and
   calls, then competitor pages that visibly earn traffic.
2. **Classify each query** by intent (learn / compare / buy — see the on-page
   reference of this skill) and by value: a query's worth is what the
   visitor is trying to do, not its volume. A small buying query beats a
   huge curiosity query for a business site.
3. **Judge difficulty by reading the SERP, not a score.** Search each
   candidate and look at who ranks: all major brands and authority sites —
   out of reach for now, find a longer variant; a mix including small
   independent sites, forums, or visibly weak pages — winnable; results that
   only loosely match the query — a gap worth taking. Vendor difficulty
   numbers are estimates with unknown error; the live SERP is the actual
   competition. Never quote a volume or difficulty figure without a data
   source in hand.
4. **Cluster and map.** Group variants that show the same results page —
   they are one intent and get one page. Queries with clearly different
   results get different pages. One primary cluster per page, page shape
   matching SERP shape.
5. **Sequence by winnability.** Specific, longer queries first; they close
   the authority gap that later funds the broader targets. "Rank for the big
   head term" is a destination, not a starting plan — say so.

## Reading a SERP as evidence

Treat every results page as a data source with several readings:

- **Features present** — ads on top, shopping units, a map pack, an AI
  Overview, video carousels — each one removes clicks from the plain
  results. A first-position rank under an AI Overview and a wall of ads is
  worth far less than the owner imagines; set that expectation.
- **Result shape** — defines intent (see the on-page reference of this skill).
- **Domains ranking** — defines difficulty (above).
- **Titles of winners** — show the promise that wins the click; compete
  with a sharper honest promise, not a copy.
- **A map pack present** means Google reads local intent — the local
  playbook applies, not just the content playbook.

## Backlinks — evaluation, not accumulation

Links still function as votes of trust, weighted by the linker's own
standing. Judgment beats counting.

Judging a single link: is the linking page about a related topic; is the
link editorial (placed inside content because the author chose to) rather
than in a footer, a directory, or a paid slot; could a real person plausibly
click it; is the anchor natural language? A link failing most of these is
worthless but usually harmless.

Judging a profile: breadth of distinct referring domains matters more than
raw link counts (many links from one domain are one vote); anchors should be
mostly brand and page-name mentions — a pile of exact-match money anchors
is the classic footprint of bought links; growth should track things the
site actually did. A sudden spike of links from unrelated foreign sites is
usually spam that Google ignores on its own.

Decision rules:

- **Disavow almost never.** Reserve it for a manual action from Search
  Console or clear evidence of a past paid-link scheme. Routinely
  disavowing "toxic" links flagged by tools is busywork with real downside
  risk; the toxicity scores are vendor inventions.
- **Never recommend** buying links at scale, link networks, or automated
  outreach spam. State the risk honestly: at best ignored, at worst a
  manual action that costs more than the links ever earned.
- **What earns links**: original data or research others must cite, free
  tools, genuinely definitive resources, expert commentary given to
  journalists, and being the primary source for anything. Frame link
  earning as publishing things worth citing, plus telling the right people
  they exist.
- **Competitor gap reading**: pages linking to several competitors but not
  to the owner's site are the warmest outreach list that exists.

Cannot be concluded: that any specific link caused a rank change, or a
site's "authority" as a single number. DA/DR are third-party estimates —
useful for rough sorting, never to be quoted as Google's view.

## Rank tracking — interpretation discipline

Rank data is the only reading that speaks about rankings directly, and the
easiest to over-read.

- **Volatility is baseline.** Positions wobble daily without cause worth
  naming. Judge trends across weeks; react to sustained moves, and to moves
  that coincide with a known change (a site edit, a confirmed Google
  update, a competitor relaunch) — and even then say "coincides with", not
  "because".
- **Segment before concluding.** Split branded from non-branded (branded
  rank is loyalty, not SEO), and split by intent. An average across all
  tracked queries hides everything; averages moving because one big query
  moved is the usual false story.
- **Position and clicks diverge** when SERP features change. Rank held,
  clicks fell, impressions held: the layout changed above the result (AI
  Overview, more ads) — not a site problem, and site fixes will not undo
  it. Check the live SERP before diagnosing.
- **Survivorship bias**: the tracked list is what someone chose to track.
  Wins on untracked queries and losses outside the list are invisible.
  Cross-check against Search Console's full query list periodically.
- **Location and personalization**: rank differs by city and history. The
  owner's own browser is anecdote; neutral rank data is the record. For
  local businesses, rank only means rank-in-a-place.
- Tie rank to sessions and funnels before celebrating: a rank win that
  produces no sessions is a features-took-the-clicks story; sessions
  without funnel progress is an intent-mismatch story.

## Local SEO basics

When searches carry local intent (a map pack appears), the Google Business
Profile competes separately from the website, on three broad forces:
relevance (does the profile say what the searcher wants), distance (not
influenceable — say so honestly), and prominence (reviews, links, overall
reputation).

Priority order for a local business:

1. **Profile completeness and category.** The primary category is the
   strongest lever on the profile; wrong category, wrong searches. Fill
   services, hours, photos (real ones, added regularly), and keep them
   current.
2. **Reviews**: a steady flow beats a burst; the reply habit is visible to
   every reader and signals a live business. Never fake or gate reviews —
   ask every happy customer, make the link effortless.
3. **Consistency**: the business name, address, and phone should match
   across the site, the profile, and major directories. Fix the big
   inconsistencies; do not buy bulk citation packages.
4. **The site still matters**: a page per service and per location — each
   with real, distinct content (staff, photos, directions, local
   specifics), not a template with the city name swapped, which is doorway
   territory.
5. **Service-area businesses** hide the address and set service areas;
   storefronts show the address. Never fake an address for a map presence;
   suspensions follow.

## AI search and GEO (generative engine optimization)

Assistants (AI Overviews, ChatGPT search, Perplexity, Copilot) answer
directly and cite a handful of sources. What raises the odds of being
cited, in current understanding — this field is young, say so:

- **Be quotable**: direct, self-contained answers under clear headings
  (the same property that wins snippets). Pages that state facts plainly
  get lifted; pages that meander get skipped.
- **Be crawlable by AI bots**: check robots.txt for blocks on the AI
  crawlers (GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot and kin) and decide the
  tradeoff deliberately — blocked means invisible to those answers.
  `llms.txt` is an emerging convention, not a standard; fine to add, no
  miracles to expect.
- **Be a consistent entity**: same facts about the business everywhere
  (site, profiles, directories); assistants cross-check and hedge when
  sources disagree.
- **Structured data** (organization, product, FAQ, how-to schema) helps
  machines parse claims; it is plumbing, not magic.
- **Measurement is immature.** Some referrer data exists; much
  assistant-driven discovery arrives untagged or as direct visits. The
  honest check is asking the assistants the money questions and seeing who
  they cite — repeat it periodically like rank tracking. Expect the
  impressions-hold-clicks-fall pattern as answers absorb simple queries;
  the durable defense is content whose value survives being summarized —
  tools, data, and things worth clicking through to.

## What Ad Rank and landing-page quality share with SEO

Google Ads ranks ads by bid times quality; the quality side (Quality Score)
has three published components: expected clickthrough rate, ad relevance,
and landing-page experience. The third is SEO's territory: page matches the
promise that was clicked, loads fast, works on phones, easy to navigate,
transparent about the business. Fix the landing page and both channels pay.

Practical crossovers:

- A low landing-page-experience grade in Ads is a free page-quality
  diagnosis that applies to organic too — and vice versa: a page that
  cannot rank organically usually costs more per click in Ads.
- The Ads search-terms report is the best keyword-research source in this
  file — real queries with real conversion outcomes attached.
- Run Ads on a query the site also ranks for to measure with money what
  the organic listing is worth — and to hold the SERP while organic builds.
- The differences to state plainly: Ads is an auction rerun on every
  search — pay stops, traffic stops; organic compounds slowly and
  persists. They are complements, not substitutes; neither "does the other
  one's job".

## Evidence limits for this file

Rank causes cannot be proven, only correlated. Search volumes, difficulty
scores, and competitor traffic must come from a live data source or not be
stated at all. Assistant citation behavior must be checked, not assumed.
Link value cannot be reduced to a vendor score. Where a claim in an answer
depends on any of these, name the reading that would settle it — Search
Console, the Ads search-terms report, rank data, or a live SERP check — and
offer that check as the next step.
